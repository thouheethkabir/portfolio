Café & Restaurant · Brand, website & launch

Ojas Café

Palani’s first air-conditioned café. I worked on this one end to end — brand identity, menu, website, the systems that run the counter, and the launch itself.

The brief

A new café opening in Palani, a temple town in Tamil Nadu. Nothing existed yet — no name treatment, no menu design, no website, no systems. The opening date was fixed and everything had to be ready before it.

This wasn’t a website project that grew. It was a launch, and the website was one part of it.

Positioning

The strategy we settled on was accessible premium: the trending metro-café food people in Palani see on Instagram but can’t get locally — loaded fries, wings, waffles, brownie shakes, kunafa — at tier-3 prices, in an air-conditioned room.

That decision drove everything downstream. The audience is college students, young adults and families visiting the temple, so the brand had to feel warm, loud and playful rather than minimalist-luxury. Premium feel, not premium price. Every piece of copy is written for a twenty-year-old: short, confident, punchy.

Brand identity

The mark is a bitten donut — an O with a bite taken out of it. A bite mark reads as appetite and enjoyment; the closed circle reads as completeness and togetherness. It sits with the Ojas wordmark and a small “Café” descriptor.

The tagline is “Take the Bite!” — three words, imperative, and it works equally as a headline, a footer sign-off and a page title.

  • Ojas Orange #DF3805 for headings, CTAs and the bite mark
  • Deep Brown #733108 for item names and body text
  • Cream #F6EFE4 as the default surface, carried from the printed menu
  • Climate Crisis for display type, Akaya Kanadaka for everything else
  • Torn orange borders, hand-drawn food illustrations and dotted leader lines

Neither typeface has a bold weight, so hierarchy is built from size, colour, case and letter-spacing instead of faux-bold — a constraint that kept the whole system disciplined.

The menu

133 dishes, structured as data rather than design. The printed menu card became the design system for everything else: cream surfaces, torn orange frame, category headings with a line illustration beside them, item rows with a dotted leader running to the price.

On the website that same menu lives in a single plain file. Adding a dish or changing a price means editing one line, which is the difference between a menu that stays current and one that quietly goes stale.

The website

Four pages — home, menu, gallery and visit — hand-coded in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step, deployed as static files. Most visitors arrive from the Instagram bio link on a phone, often on 4G, so it was designed at 390px first and kept deliberately light.

A settings block at the top of one script holds the phone number, WhatsApp link, Google Maps link, address, hours and the opening offer. Fill those in once and every Call button, every Directions link, the footer and the Open now badge update across all four pages together.

  • Mobile-first layouts built for Instagram traffic
  • Menu preview on the home page, full menu on its own page
  • Photo and video gallery
  • LocalBusiness structured data for Google — hours, location, cuisine, phone
  • A holding page that ran until opening night
  • A plain-English guide so the owner can maintain it unaided

Operations, billing and loyalty

A café doesn’t open because the website is finished. The counter has to work on day one, so I helped choose and set up the stack that runs it:

  • Petpooja — point of sale and billing, with the full menu and pricing loaded in
  • Razorpay POS — card and UPI payments at the counter
  • Reelo — loyalty and WhatsApp CRM, so a first visit can turn into a second
  • Google Business Profile — the listing most local customers actually find first
  • Instagram — @ojas_cafe, the main discovery channel

Reelo matters more than it looks. A café in a town this size lives on people coming back, and a loyalty programme wired into WhatsApp reaches customers where they already are — without buying ads to reach people who have already visited once.

Marketing and launch

The launch ran in three stages rather than one. A pre-launch card campaign went out through the owner’s existing pharmacy — an audience already in hand, which is far cheaper than buying attention cold. That was followed by a soft launch to test the kitchen at real volume, and then a grand inauguration.

The website and the holding page were sequenced to match: the holding page carried the opening date and the Instagram link until opening night, then handed over to the full site.

What’s next

The work didn’t stop at opening. In progress is a cinematic scroll experience for the home page built from real footage of the café, including a reconstructed 3D walk from the entrance to the counter — so someone deciding where to go can see the room before they arrive.
